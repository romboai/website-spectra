## Target user

CRO, CDMO and in-house analytical teams who verify proposed structures and chase unexpected signals beside a known major component.

## Specific problem

The chemist who ranked the candidates is not always the chemist who signs the report. When the reasoning lives in a notebook, the next reviewer starts from zero.

## Existing workflow

Process in TopSpin, Delta or Mnova. Predict, search a library, and write the discrepancy list by hand. Impurity work waits for enough 13C.

## Where time is lost

Close analogues and isomers. Low-level impurities with three overlapping protons and no usable 13C. Re-explaining the same shortlist on the next shift.

## How Spectra helps

Spectra ranks proposed structures and impurity hypotheses from 1H and 13C, with match, 13C RMSD and per-shift residuals. Reviewers can agree or push back on the same evidence trail.

## Data typically required

1H and 13C of the sample or enriched impurity; solvent and field; the proposed structure when this is verification.

## Output

A ranked shortlist and the signals nothing explains — often the real impurity or a second component.

## Limitations

Spectra will not construct a structure from three overlapping protons. It does not apply ICH identification thresholds or a validated impurity method. The chemist remains responsible for the report.

## Recommended validation

Authentic overlay or a standard for the accepted structure. Escalate when residuals stay large or two analogues cannot be separated.
